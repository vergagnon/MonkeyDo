# Solo-Operator Business Ideas (Claude as the Operator)

Brainstorm: businesses where an AI agent can genuinely build the product, run the
operations, do the marketing, and handle support — end to end — with the human
owner acting only as director, bank account, and legal entity.

## What "I can run it alone" actually requires

Before the ideas, an honest filter. A business is AI-operable end-to-end when:

1. **The product is digital.** No inventory, no shipping, no physical labor.
2. **Sales are self-serve.** Checkout via Stripe/Lemon Squeezy — no sales calls,
   no demos, no negotiation.
3. **Support fits in text.** Email and chat tickets, answerable from docs and
   code I wrote myself.
4. **Marketing compounds without ad spend.** SEO, programmatic content, app-store
   distribution, or free tools — channels where output volume (my strength)
   beats budget.
5. **Maintenance is code and content**, not people management or vendor calls.
6. **Low regulatory surface.** No health, finance, or legal advice; no data that
   triggers heavy compliance.

What the human still owns, non-negotiably: the legal entity, the Stripe/bank
account, domain and app-store accounts, spending approvals, and final judgment
calls. Realistic autonomy is 90–95%, not 100% — think "general manager who
checks in with the owner weekly."

One genuine enabler in this environment: I can schedule recurring sessions
(daily/weekly routines) that wake up, check metrics and support inboxes, fix
bugs, publish content, and report back. "Maintain and scale by myself" is
mechanically real here, not a metaphor.

## The ideas

### Tier 1 — Best fit (recommended starting points)

**1. Niche vertical SaaS: quote & estimate builder for custom makers**
Custom furniture makers, cabinet shops, and trades quote bespoke work in
spreadsheets and lose margin to guesswork. A focused tool — materials + board
footage + finish + labor → branded PDF quote, deposit invoice, e-signature —
is a real pain reliever at $29–79/mo. **Why this one:** you already live in
this industry through ref., so positioning, vocabulary, and the first ten
customers are unusually cheap to get. Bilingual FR/EN out of the box targets
an underserved Quebec/Canada market. I build it, run support, write the SEO
content ("how to price custom furniture"), and ship features weekly.
*Autonomy ~90%. Revenue ceiling: $5–50k MRR. Defensibility: niche depth.*

**2. Shopify or Klaviyo app**
App stores solve the hardest solo-business problem — distribution. Merchants
search, install, and pay through the store; no marketing engine needed to get
first revenue. Candidate wedge (from your own world): a Klaviyo add-on that
turns project photos + notes into on-brand campaign drafts, or a Shopify app
for made-to-order/lead-time management (deposit handling, production queue,
"ready in X weeks" flows — everything standard Shopify does badly for bespoke
sellers). *Autonomy ~90% (owner holds the partner account). Revenue ceiling:
$2–100k MRR. Defensibility: reviews compound.*

**3. Programmatic SEO directory + free-tool cluster**
A niche directory (e.g., "find a custom furniture maker / woodworker near you"
for North America) surrounded by free calculators (board-foot, wood movement,
finish coverage, project cost estimator). Revenue: featured listings
($20–50/mo), lead fees, affiliate. This is the purest "AI scales it" play —
thousands of pages, continuous refresh, zero marginal cost. Also composts
nicely: the directory audience later becomes the customer list for idea #1.
*Autonomy ~95%. Revenue ceiling: $1–15k MRR per directory, and it's repeatable
across niches. Defensibility: low individually, real as a portfolio.*

**2b. ⭐ Deposit app with flat pricing (no % of sale) — deep dive**
Verified landscape (Aug 2026): every incumbent meters usage. Depo charges
$9–149/mo *plus* 1%→0.5% + $0.10–0.20 per transaction. Downpay ($29/mo, 5.0★
category leader) has no %, but caps processed order value at $5k/mo (Starter)
and $25k/mo (Pro, $99), with per-order overage beyond — a usage tax in
flat-fee clothing. Partialy only drops its commission at an Enterprise tier.
Nobody sells true flat unlimited at a normal price.

Key economics: deposit apps never touch the money — payments run through the
merchant's own Shopify Payments; the app orchestrates terms and charges the
vaulted card for the balance. Per-merchant costs are fixed, so % fees are pure
margin capture. A flat-price entrant ($39–49/mo, unlimited order value)
concedes nothing structurally.

Positioning: "One price. No percentages, no caps, no overages — ever."
Target the merchants usage pricing hurts most: few orders, huge values
(custom furniture, jewelry, bridal, art commissions, equipment). Marketing =
comparison pages (Downpay/Depo alternatives) + a free "what do deposit-app
fees cost you at your AOV" calculator.

Caveats: (1) price is a wedge, not a moat — incumbents can uncap; durable
moat = reviews + made-to-order workflow depth + FR/EN. (2) Build a free
"migrate your open balances" flow — switching friction is the real barrier to
poaching. (3) Shopify is slowly adding native deposits; stay valuable at the
workflow layer. (4) Built on selling-plan API + vaulted payment mandates;
app review / Built-for-Shopify adds a few weeks.

Math: flat $49/mo → ~170 merchants = $100k ARR. Incumbent review counts
(~90 each) suggest low-thousands installs — a winnable solo-scale market.

### Tier 2 — Solid, situational

**4. Productized reporting service: Meta Ads analysis for small e-commerce.**
Weekly automated deep-dive reports (breakdown-effect-aware diagnosis, creative
fatigue flags, action list) at $99–299/mo. You already run Meta ads, so you can
validate the report quality yourself. Needs API access per client (light
onboarding friction) but zero ongoing human labor.

**5. Paid niche dataset / API.** Collect, clean, and serve data nobody else
maintains — e.g., a lumber & sheet-goods price index scraped weekly from
suppliers, sold to makers and estimating tools (including idea #1). Small but
extremely sticky revenue; near-total autonomy.

**6. Digital asset shop.** Notion/spreadsheet templates for workshop management,
Klaviyo email template packs, website themes for makers. Sold on Lemon
Squeezy/Gumroad. Fast to launch (weeks), evergreen, but a lower ceiling —
better as a funnel and credibility layer for Tier 1 ideas than as the main
business.

**7. AI-curated niche newsletter.** Daily/weekly curation in a commercial niche
(e.g., e-commerce operators in Canada, or the maker economy), monetized via
sponsorships and classifieds once past ~5k subscribers. I can research, write,
and schedule every issue; sponsorship sales are the one part that wants a human
touch early on.

### Tier 3 — Considered and de-prioritized (and why)

- **Affiliate content sites:** AI answers in search results are eating exactly
  this traffic. High volatility, declining moat.
- **Generic AI-wrapper tools** (summarizers, chat-with-PDF): crowded, no
  distribution edge, price races to zero.
- **Anything with human-in-the-loop fulfillment** (agencies, tutoring,
  concierge services): breaks the autonomy requirement — scaling means hiring.

## Recommended sequence

**Phase 1 (this month):** Ship #3 (directory + calculators). Cheapest test of
the whole loop — I build, deploy, and start SEO compounding while we decide on
the bigger bet. **Phase 2:** Use what the directory teaches us about the
audience to build #1 (quote SaaS) or #2 (app), whichever the early signals
favor. **Phase 3:** I run both on scheduled routines — nightly ops checks,
weekly feature releases, monthly P&L summary to you — and we clone the
directory playbook into a second niche.

Owner setup checklist (one-time, ~half a day): business entity decision, Stripe
account, domain(s), hosting account (Vercel/Railway), and — if idea #2 —
a Shopify Partner account.
